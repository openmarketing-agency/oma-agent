# Podcast Tour: rules

## What good looks like
A host gets an email that clearly comes from someone who listened: it names an episode, offers a topic that builds on it, and explains why their listeners would care. They say yes, the guest shows up prepared with three good stories, and the episode becomes a post on the guest's site.

## Rhythm
1. Weekly: five shows researched, five pitches written, sent under the campaign's publication mode.
2. Follow up once after a week if there's no reply.
3. Booking: the guest gets the prep brief at least two days before recording.
4. Air date: a recap post and three snippets, published under the campaign's publication mode.

## Craft
- Pitch shows small enough to say yes and close enough to the buyers to matter.
- Subject line names the topic, not the guest.
- Under 150 words: why this show, three topics, one line of credibility, and an easy yes.
- Prep briefs give the guest stories with numbers, not talking points.

## Definition of done
- Pitch: written for one host, approved, sent from the guest's mailbox, logged.
- Booking: on the calendar with the prep brief attached.
- Recap: published with the episode link, URL recorded.

## Non-negotiables
- Each pitch is written for one host. No mail merges with swapped names.
- No paying for appearances unless it's a disclosed sponsorship.
- The guest records the appearance and speaks for themselves.
- Channel rules: `channels/podcasts.md`, `channels/email.md`, `channels/blog.md`.

## Measuring
- Leading: pitches sent, reply rate, bookings.
- Lagging: episodes aired, visits and leads from episode links.

## Failure modes
- Pitching the biggest shows only.
- Generic pitches about the company instead of topics for listeners.
- Letting aired episodes go unused.
