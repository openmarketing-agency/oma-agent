# Abandoned Cart Recovery: rules

## What good looks like
A shopper leaves a jacket in the cart. An hour later they get an email showing that jacket, in their size, with a review from someone their height, and one button back to the cart. The next day, an answer to the usual hesitation such as fit, returns or shipping. The last email, if the store allows it, offers free shipping. They buy, and the emails stop.

## Rhythm
1. Merchandiser sets the product rules; flow-writer writes the emails.
2. After approval, the publisher builds the flow with consent and stop rules and turns it on.
3. Two tests a month: subject lines, timing, the objection answered, or the offer.
4. Weekly review: recovery rate, revenue, unsubscribes and complaints.

## Craft
- Email 1 at about 1 hour: their items, no discount.
- Email 2 at about 24 hours: answer the main objection, with fit, returns, shipping or reviews.
- Email 3 at about 48 to 72 hours: the offer, if any, with a real expiry.
- Show only items in stock in their size. Never show a price that differs from the store.
- One call to action per email.

## Definition of done
- Every email in the flow is approved and built with consent rules, stop-on-purchase and unsubscribe.
- Test sends checked on desktop and mobile with real product data.
- Flow turned on and its state recorded.

## Non-negotiables
- Email only shoppers whose recorded consent allows it.
- Stop the flow the moment the order is placed.
- Discounts within the store's limits, with honest expiry dates.
- Channel rules: `channels/email.md`.

## Measuring
- Leading: clicks per email, unsubscribe and complaint rates.
- Lagging: recovered orders and revenue, and repeat purchases from the cross-sell.

## Failure modes
- Leading with a discount and training shoppers to wait for it.
- Showing items that sold out.
- Never testing after launch.
