# SEO Blog Engine: rules

## What good looks like
Two useful articles go live every week, each answering one search intent better than what ranks today, with something only this business can add. Pages that slip get rewritten before they fall off page one.

## Rhythm
1. Monday, orchestrator: pick next week's topics from the keyword map and Search Console, and queue them.
2. Researcher: a brief per topic within a day.
3. Writer: a draft per brief within two days.
4. The writer applies Humanizer and saves each finished article for the campaign's publication workflow. The publisher delivers the approved revision, requests indexing, and records the URL.
5. Weekly review: compare 28-day windows, pick refreshes, adjust the map.

## Craft
- One page per intent. Check the existing site first, and refresh rather than duplicate.
- For an authorized edit to an existing article, update the specified fields in place and verify the live result. Do not treat a metadata edit as a new publication.
- Lead with the answer. Use the searcher's words in the title and first paragraph.
- Every page carries at least one first-hand element: a screenshot, a product number, a worked example or a quote from the team.
- Internal links from at least two related pages, and one clear call to action.
- Comparison pages are honest about where the competitor is better.

## Definition of done
- Before handoff, the writer uses `humanizer` to polish the draft while preserving search intent, sourced claims, exact quotes, and links.
- Every fact has a source, and nothing is invented.
- Title, meta description, slug, schema and internal links are set.
- Published, indexing requested, and the live URL recorded on the task.

## Non-negotiables
- No pages made only to rank, and no near-duplicates with swapped keywords.
- No invented statistics, quotes, authors or reviews.
- Channel rules: `channels/blog.md`, and `channels/site.md` for pages outside the blog.

## Measuring
- Leading: pages published against plan, impressions and average position for target clusters.
- Lagging: non-brand clicks and organic conversions.
- Judge a cluster after 8 to 12 weeks, not after one article.

## Failure modes
- Writing about what the company wants to say instead of what people search.
- Publishing thin pages to hit the cadence.
- Never refreshing, so early wins decay.
