# Playbook example

A made-up brand, to show the shape and the depth. The foundation is short and comes from the research. The strategy is the marketer's own thinking, built on that foundation, and it's what campaigns are designed from. Never copy this one.

## A complete playbook

```yaml
id: playbook
name: Hilltop Tea Playbook
description: Win home tea drinkers with the freshest, easiest way into loose leaf, and use cafés as tasting rooms for the subscription.
strategy: |-
  What the product is
  Hilltop sells small-batch loose-leaf tea from named estates, shipped within weeks of harvest. It earns money from a monthly subscription, entered through a five-tea sampler, and from wholesale supply to three independent cafés. Marketing runs on the online store, Instagram and a newsletter. There is no blog, and attribution from cafés doesn't exist yet.

  Positioning
  Fresh loose-leaf tea, weeks from harvest, picked to your taste. Home drinkers get a monthly box they can skip or pause any month; cafés get a tea menu customers ask about, with staff training and table cards.

  What success looks like
  Grow active subscribers, with café wholesale as both a second income and a way new drinkers first taste the tea.

  Who we market to
  - Home tea drinkers moving on from bags: want better tea without fuss, and need to see that loose leaf is easy and fairly priced per cup. They arrive through Instagram, café table cards and gift boxes.
  - Independent café owners: want tea their customers ask for, and need margin, simple reordering and staff training. They arrive through referrals from current partners.

  ## Competitors
  - Supermarket tea: cheap and convenient. Hilltop wins on freshness.
  - Large tea subscriptions: wide range and frequent discounts. Hilltop wins on named estates and weeks-from-harvest shipping.
  - Other café suppliers: established accounts. Hilltop wins on training and table cards that bring customers back to the café.

  ## Strategy
  Hilltop wins on freshness and guidance, never on price. Supermarkets and big subscriptions own cheap and wide; Hilltop owns fresh and easy, the brand that makes starting loose leaf simple and keeps it interesting.

  The growth engine is the first taste. Most people decide to switch after tasting good tea, so Hilltop's job is to create tastings and turn them into a subscription. Cafés are tasting rooms: every partner café serves the tea and sends its customers to the sampler. The sampler turns a taste into a habit. Subscribers bring friends and suggest cafés, and each new café creates more first tastes.

  Hilltop stays out of discounting, marketplaces and supermarket shelves, which would trade away freshness and the direct relationship the subscription depends on.

  ## Priorities
  ### 1. Make the sampler the front door
  The sampler is the lowest-risk way to try loose leaf, and it's the step between a first taste and a subscription.
  - What needs to happen: the sampler is the main call to action everywhere; every sampler includes rating cards; the first subscription box is built from those ratings; emails reach sampler buyers on the days they're likely to brew.
  - Channels: store, Instagram, newsletter.
  - Depends on: rating cards and the first-box emails.
  - Success: sampler orders and the share that become subscribers.

  ### 2. Turn cafés into tasting rooms
  Every cup served in a partner café is a free tasting, and today none of them leads anywhere measurable.
  - What needs to happen: each café gets table cards with its own sampler link; subscribers can suggest a café, and those suggestions come first in outreach; café owners get a tasting box, a margin sheet and staff training; every partner is asked for one introduction.
  - Channels: partner cafés, direct outreach, the store's suggest-a-café link.
  - Depends on: café-tagged links.
  - Success: partner cafés with repeat orders, and subscribers credited to each café.

  ### 3. Give subscribers a reason to stay
  Freshness is the promise, so each box should feel like news from the estates.
  - What needs to happen: the newsletter tells each harvest's story; every box explains what's in it and how to brew it; long-term subscribers get first access to limited teas.
  - Channels: newsletter, Instagram, the box itself.
  - Success: subscribers still active after three months.

  ### 4. Start owning beginner search
  Beginners search for how to brew loose leaf and which tea to start with, and Hilltop has nothing there yet.
  - What needs to happen: a beginner hub with brewing guides, each ending in the sampler.
  - Channels: blog, Instagram.
  - Depends on: a blog on the store.
  - Success: organic visits to the guides and sampler orders from them.

  Channels and their roles
  - Core: the store, Instagram, the newsletter and partner cafés.
  - Supporting: search, once the blog exists.
  - Sales: direct outreach to cafés.
  - Not used yet: paid social, until café and sampler attribution works; marketplaces, which hide the customer relationship.

  ## Messaging
  - Core: fresh loose-leaf tea, weeks from harvest, picked to your taste.
  - Home drinkers: café-quality tea at home in five minutes, starting with a sampler.
  - Cafés: tea your customers ask about, with training and cards that bring them back to you.
  - Proof: harvest dates on every pouch, named estates, the sampler itself.
  - Objections: loose leaf is a hassle, so the sampler includes a brewing card and an infuser; it's expensive, so compare the price per cup with café tea.

  Measuring progress
  Main: active subscribers at month end, because the subscription is where the business earns.

  - Sampler: orders, rating cards returned, first-box conversion.
  - Cafés: partners with repeat orders, subscribers credited to each café.
  - Retention: subscribers active after three months.
  - Search: organic visits and sampler orders from guides.

  Café-tagged links and store analytics need to be in place first. Targets follow a baseline.

  ## Campaigns
  1. Sampler to subscription (priority 1): the sampler sells but leads nowhere. First: rating cards and the first-box emails.
  2. Café tasting rooms (priority 2; extends the existing café outreach campaign): three cafés serve Hilltop with no cards or attribution. First: café-tagged links and table cards.
  3. Harvest stories (priority 3): the newsletter exists but tells no story. First: a harvest letter for the next box.
  4. Beginner brewing hub (priority 4): no blog yet. First: set up the blog and the first five guides.

  Revisit the strategy when the sampler, subscription economics, café partnerships or primary conversion path materially changes.
```

## Facts are not a strategy

Listing what the research says doesn't tell a campaign what to do:

```
## Marketing plan
- Channels: store, Instagram, newsletter, cafés.
- Content: brewing guides, reels.
```

A strategy makes a choice and explains the mechanism:

```
## Strategy
The growth engine is the first taste. Most people decide to switch after tasting good tea, so
Hilltop's job is to create tastings and turn them into a subscription. Cafés are tasting rooms...
```

## Strategy is yours, facts are not

A strategic choice built on the research is the point:

```
Hilltop wins on freshness and guidance, never on price.
```

A fact the research doesn't hold is invented, however plausible:

```
Most subscribers are students who found loose leaf on social media.
```

And timing is never set: say what needs to happen and what depends on what, not when.
